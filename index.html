Six ideas behind the makeover

Every row says who and what

"Follow up on a call", repeated down the list, becomes "Call Dana back about the payroll split · Summit Ridge Framing · Workers comp". You can act without opening the item.

Show the agents working

Covera's best moment is an agent filling six portals at once. Today it is invisible. A live chip, progress bars and a plain step log make it felt.

One primary action per screen

Rows with four equal controls (status menu, Add, menu, remove) become one clear next step, with the rest in a quiet menu.

Status is a word and a shape

Every status pill carries an icon and a plain word, so it reads in grayscale and for color-blind users. Dropdowns stop pretending to be badges.

Tables that fit

No more sideways scroll on a laptop. Empty columns ("–" all the way down) fold away; codes like AUTOP and PRJ-… become words.

Glass for what floats, paper for what you read

The menu, top bar, assistant and search float as liquid glass. Work surfaces stay near-opaque so text keeps full contrast.

Ten fixes that lift every screen

  1. Give every item a subject.Task, activity and opportunity titles should name the person and the outcome.
  2. Fit tables to the screen.Fold empty columns, wrap secondary data under the name, and stop horizontal scroll at 1440 px.
  3. One primary button per view.Make the next step obvious; move the rest into a menu.
  4. Make statuses read-only pills.Change status from the record or a menu, not from a badge that looks like a label.
  5. Replace codes with words.AUTOP, bop, PRJ-… and RUN-… belong in a details drawer.
  6. Stack fewer banners.One notice at a time, each with its own fix button.
  7. Retire page-dimming tours.Use a small hint the first time; never cover the page that is trying to explain itself.
  8. Show agents at work everywhere.A live chip in the top bar and progress where the work is.
  9. Bring the brand inside.The website's forest and lime are stronger than the app's muted green and orange dot.
  10. Design for one hand.Every screen in the prototype works at phone width with a slide-in menu.
